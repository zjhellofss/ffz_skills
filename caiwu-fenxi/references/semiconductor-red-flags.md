# Semiconductor Validation and Red-Flag Checklist

These signals are investigation prompts, not fraud allegations or automatic negative conclusions. Cite evidence, consider management explanations, and state what remains unknown.

## Business and Technology Status

- The company uses `breakthrough`, `domestic substitution`, `customer recognition`, `validation`, or `mass production` without quantifying orders, revenue, shipments, or accepted customers.
- Tape-out, sample, validation, pilot delivery, shipment, installation, acceptance, and recognized revenue are conflated.
- Announced capacity, installed capacity, qualified capacity, and effective output are conflated.
- Market share, yield, technical performance, or customer identity is inferred from non-authoritative sources.
- R&D-stage products are presented as current revenue drivers.

## Revenue, Orders, and Customers

- Revenue grows materially faster than cash collection, while receivables, notes receivable, contract assets, or unbilled amounts rise.
- Equipment shipments or goods shipped/not accepted rise but acceptance and revenue do not follow within the disclosed normal cycle.
- Backlog includes cancellable orders, framework agreements, tax, or multi-year amounts without reconciliation.
- Contract liabilities rise or fall sharply without a corresponding explanation of orders, delivery, or acceptance.
- Distributor/channel revenue rises while sell-through, returns, rebates, price protection, or channel inventory is unclear.
- Billed distributor concentration is described as end-customer concentration or dispersion without end-customer evidence.
- A new major customer drives growth but concentration, credit terms, related-party status, or sustainability is unclear.
- Customer qualification is described without evidence of volume supply or repeat orders.

## Inventory and Obsolescence

- Inventory or inventory days grows faster than revenue/cost of sales through multiple periods.
- Work in process moves into finished goods without matching sales.
- Fabless inventory rises while product prices fall, end demand weakens, or a replacement generation launches.
- Equipment finished goods, demo tools, or goods shipped/not accepted increase faster than accepted revenue.
- Materials inventory with shelf-life or contamination risk rises without corresponding provisions.
- Inventory write-down rates fall despite aging, price pressure, weak demand, or technology migration.
- Large write-down reversals or disposals materially support profit.

Always separate raw materials/wafers, work in process, outsourced processing, finished goods, goods shipped/not accepted, demo tools, spares, and channel inventory when disclosed.

## Capacity, Capex, and Depreciation

- Large announced investment is presented as completed capex or available capacity.
- Construction-in-progress and equipment prepayments rise for several periods without transfer, production, or impairment explanation.
- Transfers to fixed assets accelerate, but depreciation guidance or margin impact is absent.
- Capacity expands while utilization, output, or demand weakens.
- Capitalized interest, development cost, or trial-production expenditure rises materially.
- Operating cash flow cannot fund capex for an extended period and financing dependence increases.
- Government-funded projects carry performance, employment, production, or clawback obligations that are not discussed.

## Margin and Earnings Quality

- Gross-margin improvement is attributed to product mix, localization, yield, or utilization without disclosed evidence.
- Profit depends materially on grants, tax benefits, fair-value gains, asset disposals, or impairment reversals.
- R&D investment rises but R&D expense does not, without an explanation of capitalization or acquired intangibles.
- Depreciation, warranty, inventory provisions, or credit-loss provisions fall despite operational expansion or weakening conditions.
- Adjusted/non-GAAP profit repeatedly excludes acquisition amortization, share-based compensation, inventory charges, restructuring, or other economically recurring items.
- Profit rises while operating cash flow and free cash flow weaken.

## Supply Chain, Policy, and Geography

- One foundry, substrate, EDA/IP provider, key component, raw material, or logistics route represents a material single-source dependency.
- High supplier concentration is called `single-source` without evidence of exclusivity, no qualified alternative, or switching constraints.
- Non-cancellable purchase or capacity commitments exceed visible demand without downside protection.
- Export controls, entity restrictions, licensing requirements, local-content rules, tariffs, or sanctions affect products, customers, suppliers, or service capability.
- Geographic revenue is used as a proxy for customer nationality or end use without evidence.
- Localization claims refer only to supplier location, assembly, or ownership and do not establish technical or economic substitution.
- Overseas revenue grows while collection, tax, distributor, compliance, or FX exposure is insufficiently disclosed.

Verify current policy and restriction facts using authoritative, dated sources. Do not infer that a restriction applies merely because a product is advanced or semiconductor-related.

## Government Support and Related Parties

- Government grants, tax incentives, subsidized financing, or capital injections form a material portion of profit or cash inflow.
- Grant income is treated as recurring operating profitability without separating type and conditions.
- Other income, non-operating income, deferred-income balance/movement, cash receipts, and non-recurring grant classifications are added without a reconciliation proving they do not overlap.
- Industrial funds, local governments, controlling shareholders, customers, or suppliers are related parties and transactions are material.
- Project commitments, repurchase clauses, performance guarantees, or subsidy clawbacks are omitted from the main analysis.

## Required Open Questions When Disclosure Is Insufficient

Ask only questions material to the company, such as:

- What percentage of new-product revenue comes from mass production rather than samples or pilot delivery?
- Are disclosed orders firm, cancellable, tax-inclusive, and scheduled for what delivery period?
- What triggers revenue recognition: shipment, installation, acceptance, usage, or customer resale?
- How much inventory is tied to superseded products, customer-specific configurations, or unaccepted equipment?
- Is capacity design, installed, qualified, or effective capacity, and when is economic utilization expected?
- What depreciation follows the current CIP transfer schedule?
- How much growth came from volume, ASP, mix, acquisition, FX, or low-base effects?
- What are the financial effects and compliance dependencies of current export controls or licensing rules?
- How much operating profit and cash flow remain after separately identifying grants, tax benefits, disposals, and other non-core support?

## Final Semiconductor Integrity Check

Before finalizing, confirm:

- subsector classification and revenue model are explicit;
- only applicable KPIs are used and every definition is sourced;
- commercialization stage is separated from financial contribution;
- cycle, inventory, capacity, and revenue-recognition effects are addressed;
- capex is reconciled through CIP, fixed assets, depreciation, utilization, and cash flow when data exists;
- equipment orders, shipment, acceptance, and revenue are not conflated;
- material qualification claims are not presented as stable volume supply;
- policy and market facts are current and authoritatively sourced;
- peer comparisons use compatible business and accounting definitions;
- unavailable yield, utilization, ASP, backlog, share, or customer identity is not estimated.
- every repeated conclusion keeps the same source-stated/calculated/inference classification, and the report avoids assurance-like claims that red-flag checks cannot prove.
